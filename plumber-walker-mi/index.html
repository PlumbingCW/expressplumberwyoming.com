Why winter and spring decide Walker's plumbing year

Michigan winters freeze and thaw the same pipes again and again. Water expands as it turns to ice, and a pipe in an unheated crawl space, an exterior wall, or a garage ceiling can split along a seam without any sound. The damage often shows up only when the ice melts and the water starts to flow.

That is why a burst in Walker can look like a calm afternoon problem that began in the night. If you find a wet ceiling, a bulging wall, or a pipe that has gone silent after a cold snap, treat it as an emergency and call. Our burst pipe repair crews work on a 24/7 basis because frozen pipes do not follow a schedule.

Spring brings a second wave. Snowmelt loads the ground around foundations, and a sump pump that sat idle all winter suddenly runs nonstop. Test it before the thaw by pouring water into the pit and watching the float rise. If it fails the test, our sump pump service can replace it before the first heavy melt.

Season by season: what to watch in a Walker house

Each season has its own typical failure. These are patterns, not predictions, but they help you decide what to check and when.

Late fall

Disconnect garden hoses and shut off exterior spigots from inside if your home has a shutoff for them. Check the crawl space for gaps where cold air can reach supply lines.

Deep winter

Let a faucet drip on the coldest nights if a pipe runs along an exterior wall. Open cabinet doors under sinks on outside walls so house air reaches the pipes.

Early spring thaw

Watch for damp spots on ceilings and basement walls as frozen pipes thaw. A pipe that split in January may only leak once the ice releases, which is when emergency calls spike.

Summer

Heavy rain and tree roots are the usual culprits. Slow drains and gurgling toilets can point to roots or a partial blockage in the line leaving the house.

While you wait: four moves that limit the damage in Walker

These steps are safe for most homeowners and buy time until a plumber arrives.

Find the main and close it

Turn the main shutoff clockwise until it stops. Look near where the water line enters the house, often close to the meter. If the valve is stuck, do not force it, and tell the dispatcher.

Cut power near the water

If water is near outlets, appliances or the panel, switch off the breaker for that area only if you can reach it dry. If the panel itself is wet, stay away and call your electric utility. Electrical repair is outside what we do.

Clear and contain

Move rugs, boxes and electronics out of the wet zone. Use towels and a bucket to slow the spread, and take photos for your insurer before cleanup.
