Garbage disposal repair in Wyoming starts with understanding the machine. A disposal does not have knife blades. A motor spins a flywheel with small impellers that push food against a shredder ring, and water flushes the pieces down the drain.

What the noise or symptom means

Most disposal problems announce themselves. A slow sink with a healthy motor points past the unit to a drain clog, and a drip at the faucet side belongs to faucet repair instead.

Sink backing up or disposal leaking into the cabinet

A leaking disposal can spread water across the cabinet floor and into the kitchen subfloor, and a blocked one can send wastewater back through the dishwasher line. Stop running the dishwasher, turn the disposal off, and close the sink's supply shutoffs if water is escaping.

We take these calls overnight and on weekends, and same-day appointments are offered when available when the leak is slow and you can safely bucket it until morning.

  • 1Switch off the disposal and the dishwasher.

  • 5Take a photo of the label on the disposal for the dispatcher.

Freeing a jam the safe way

Homeowners can clear many jams without a visit. These steps keep fingers away from the impellers.

  1. 1

    Cut the power

    Turn the switch off and, if you can reach it, unplug the unit under the sink.

  2. 2

    Find the hex socket

    Most disposals have a hex-shaped socket in the center on the underside. An Allen wrench turns the flywheel back and forth.

  3. 3

    Remove the obstruction

    Use tongs or pliers, never fingers, to pull out a bone, a bottle cap or whatever stuck.

  4. 4

    Press the reset

    The red reset button on the bottom pops out when the motor overheats. Wait for it to cool, then press it.

  5. 5

    Test with water

    Run cold water, switch on the unit, and listen. If it hums again or trips, stop and call.

Where code meets the drain under your sink

The trap and waste line serving a disposal fall under the 2021 Michigan Plumbing Code, based on the 2021 IPC. Repairing the unit itself is not permit work, but changing the drain arrangement or adding fixtures is.

If you are unsure which side your job lands on, call Wyoming Building Inspections. The manufacturer's instructions control mounting and wiring. Ask before you buy parts if the sink layout is changing.

Leaks at the flange, the housing and the dishwasher line

The flange is the metal ring at the sink opening, sealed with plumber's putty or a gasket and held by a mounting assembly underneath. If it loosens, water drips down the outside of the unit. Tightening or resealing the flange works if the sink and mount are sound.

A leak from the side usually means the dishwasher hose or the drain elbow. A leak from the bottom, from a hairline crack or a failed seal in the housing, means replacement. No sealant fixes a cracked body.

Repair, or buy a new disposal

Repair is realistic when

  • The jam clears and the motor spins freely.
  • The leak is at the flange or a hose.
  • The unit is otherwise quiet and strong.
  • The mounting assembly is intact.

Replace when

  • The housing is leaking from below.
  • The motor trips the reset repeatedly.
  • The grinding sound is rough or uneven.
  • The unit rusts at the base.

What never goes down the disposal

Most repeat visits come from the same few items. Keeping them out extends the unit's life.

  • ✓Grease and cooking oil, which coat the drain and harden in the pipe.
  • ✓Fibrous scraps such as celery, corn husks and artichoke leaves, which wrap around the impellers.
  • ✓Coffee grounds in large amounts, which pack into sludge in the trap.
  • ✓Starchy foods such as rice, pasta and potato peels, which swell into paste.
  • ✓Bones, fruit pits and shells, which can jam or dull the shredder ring.
  • ✓Non-food objects, including utensils and bottle caps, which cause the worst jams.

Installing a disposal where there was none

Adding a disposal means a new electrical connection, a mounting assembly in the sink and a drain connection. We handle the plumbing side. If the kitchen has no switched outlet under the sink, electrical work is a separate trade, and we will say so before scheduling. Pair this with our installation service if the kitchen is also getting a new sink or faucet.

Check your sewage system first. Homes outside the public service area around Byron Center often use private wells and septic systems, and a disposal adds solids to a septic tank. If that is your setup, ask about the effect on the tank before adding one. Our Byron Center page covers the area.
