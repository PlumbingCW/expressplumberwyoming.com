Sewer line repair in Wyoming means fixing the pipe that carries waste from your house to the city main. The repair might be a short section dug out and replaced, a liner installed from inside, or a full new run. Which one makes sense depends on where the pipe has failed and how long the damage extends.

Whose pipe is it: city side or private side

Wyoming accepts responsibility for the sanitary sewer mains and for the residential connections within the right of way. If a backup begins on that side, Public Works coordinates the repair at no cost to you. Staff are on call 24/7 for sewer backups at 616-530-7260, and the city asks you to call before you call a plumber.

If Public Works finds the problem on your private side, you hire a plumber and pay for the repair. That is where we come in. Calling the city first can save you a bill you do not owe, and it costs a few minutes on the phone.

Spot repair, lining or full replacement

Three approaches cover most failed sewer lines. Which one applies depends on camera findings, not on a guess.

Cast iron and clay from the 1950s boom

Wyoming grew fastest during the post-war 1950s, and houses from that period were commonly built with cast iron or clay sewer pipe. Both lasted a long time, but neither lasts forever. Cast iron corrodes from inside and can flake or crack, and clay is brittle and joins in short segments with many seams.

Those seams give roots places to enter, and shifting ground can pull joints apart. A pipe like that may still drain, but it will often clog more frequently. If your home is from that era and backups keep returning, the pipe may be telling you it needs repair rather than another cleaning.

Roots in the line

Root intrusion is one of the most common reasons a private sewer line fails. Roots enter at a loose joint or small crack, grow into a mat, and then catch paper and waste until the line stops. Cutting them out clears the line for a while, but the opening they used is still there.

If the pipe is otherwise sound, a liner can seal those joints from the inside and keep roots out. If the pipe is cracked through, a section replacement is more honest. Our hydro jetting work can clear the roots first so the camera sees the true condition of the pipe.

How we repair a failing sewer line

Pipe lining

A resin-soaked liner is pulled through the old pipe and cured in place. It forms a smooth inner pipe without a long trench, and it works best when the host pipe is round and still intact.

Epoxy coating for short sections

A thin epoxy layer can seal small leaks and rough interiors. It suits limited problems, not a structurally failed pipe.

Open-cut spot repair

We dig down to the damaged section, cut it out and splice in new pipe. This is a direct fix when only a few feet have failed.

Cast iron section replacement

Where cast iron is corroded through, the damaged stretch is replaced with modern pipe and tied to the sound part of the line.

Full line replacement

When the pipe has collapsed along its length, a new line is the dependable answer.

Permits and Kentwood-side questions

In Wyoming, replacing drain piping needs a plumbing permit from the city's Building Inspections office before the work starts. That is something we plan for, and it means a repair should not begin on a handshake. The city also inspects the work after.

Neighboring Kentwood runs its own permit office, which takes plumbing permit applications online. If your property sits on that side of the line, our Kentwood plumbing page explains how we cover it, and we can tell you which office applies to your address.

A short, honest word on septic

Almost everyone in Wyoming is on city sewer, so septic is the exception here. It applies in some outlying areas, such as parts of Byron Center, where homes may use private septic systems. In Kent County, the county's septic and well program issues permits for on-site systems, so that is where such work begins.

If your home has a septic tank, a backup may be a full tank or a failed drain field rather than a broken sewer line. We can help with the plumbing inside the house and tell you honestly when the problem belongs to a septic specialist.
