Commercial plumbing in Wyoming means the supply lines, drains, fixtures and backflow devices serving a shop, office, restaurant or small building instead of a single household. We handle light-commercial work and say plainly where that stops.

Where light-commercial ends

We work on storefronts, offices, small restaurants, retail units and the common areas of small multi-unit properties. That means restrooms, floor drains, mop sinks, water heaters, supply lines, grease interceptors and drain stoppages.

We do not do large industrial process piping, fire sprinkler systems, medical gas, or HVAC, electrical and restoration work. If a call needs one of those, the dispatcher will tell you so on the first call instead of sending a truck that cannot finish the job.

Backed-up restroom or overflowing floor drain after hours

Sewage in a public space is a health issue as well as a repair. Close the area, put down signage, and keep anyone with open food or beverages away from the wet floor.

If wastewater is rising from a floor drain, the problem may start in the city main. Public Works keeps staff on call 24/7 for sewer backups and asks callers to phone them before a plumber at 616-530-7260. Make that call while our dispatcher takes your details, because a blockage in the main is theirs to clear and a private-side stoppage is the building owner's.

  • 2Close off the area and post a wet-floor or closed-restroom sign.
  • 3Move inventory, paper and electronics off the floor.
  • 4Photograph the water line and the affected drains for the owner and insurer.
  • 5Tell the dispatcher whether the building is occupied and how to reach the shutoff valves.

Backflow devices and the city's testing requirement

A backflow preventer sits on a water line to stop used water from flowing backward into the clean supply. Irrigation systems, boilers, fire lines, commercial kitchens and some wash stations often have one. Wyoming runs a Cross Connection Control Program, and the city requires periodic testing of all backflow devices. If your building has one, find out when it was last tested before an overdue notice finds you.

When a device fails its test, the choices are repair or replacement, and the right one depends on the device's age and whether parts are still available. Tell the dispatcher what the tag says. We will confirm what we can handle on your device and what the testing step involves before anyone is scheduled.

Replacing supply piping or a device can also trigger a plumbing permit. Wyoming Building Inspections requires permits before work starts on replacement of water supply pipes, and the permit holder schedules inspections with at least 24 hours' notice. Mention the deadline early so the permit does not become the slow part of your repair.

Restrooms, grease and drains: the repeat offenders

Cabling clears most stoppages, and our drain cleaning service covers the building lines behind them.

Restroom stoppages

Heavy use plus paper products and wipes clog shared branches fast. Cabling clears the stoppage, and a camera shows whether the line has a deeper defect.

Flush valves and fixtures

A running or weak flush often traces to worn seals, not a clog. When the valve is past saving, replacement falls under fixture installation.

Grease traps and interceptors

A full interceptor sends grease downstream into the building line. We can service interceptor-related stoppages, and we will tell you if the pumping itself is a separate hauler's job.

Floor drains and lift pumps

Floor drains that overflow usually reflect a problem further along. Where a lift pump handles the drain, a failed float or a jammed impeller can mimic a clog.

Storm and trench drains

Leaves, grit and debris pack lot drains and trench grates. Light cleaning and grate access are routine, and anything involving paving or excavation is a different contractor.

Commercial work under the Michigan code

Light-commercial plumbing in Wyoming falls under the same 2021 Michigan Plumbing Code (2021 IPC basis, effective March 12, 2024). The city's Cross Connection Control Program also requires periodic testing of backflow devices.

The owner-resident permit exception covers a dwelling, not a business, so commercial work calls for someone licensed by the State of Michigan. Keep your device test results where a landlord or inspector can find them.

Working along the 28th Street corridor and other commercial blocks

M-11 runs through the middle of the city as a 3.5-mile five-lane commercial strip, and many of the units along it sit in connected buildings. In a shared building, one tenant's backup can show up in another's floor drain, so confirm which line serves your unit before assuming the problem is yours.

Busy frontage and delivery schedules also make daytime access awkward. We can often plan the work for after closing hours, which keeps a restroom or kitchen in service during the day. We also dispatch to businesses just across the city line, including Kentwood.

For landlords and property managers

Property managers usually need three things from a plumber: a single point of contact, a clear note on whether the cause is tenant or building, and photos or a short written summary for the file. Give the dispatcher the unit, the access arrangements and who approves spending, and we will report back in that order.

A typical call looks like this: a tenant reports a slow restroom on a Friday evening, the manager cannot reach the owner, and the question is whether to wait until Monday. Call the line, describe what is happening, and we will say whether it can safely hold or needs a visit tonight.

One honest section on commercial leak detection

A steady rise in the water bill with no visible leak points to a hidden one. Shut off every fixture, check whether the meter still moves, then call. We trace supply-line leaks with listening and pressure methods, and you can read more about the process on our leak detection page.

We locate and repair the plumbing. We do not dry out or rebuild what the water damaged, so keep a restoration company in mind if ceilings or flooring got wet.
