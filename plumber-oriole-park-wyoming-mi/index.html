Oriole Park is a neighborhood in Wyoming, roughly between 36th and 44th Streets and less than a mile from US-131, according to a listing site. The same site puts the typical home build year at 1961, with ranch, split-level and Cape Cod styles on lots of about a third of an acre.

That makes it a good place to talk about everyday fixtures. This page covers kitchen and bath repairs, disposals and what to check with a lower level. These notes are general. Call (855) 575-2890 to book a visit.

Why fixtures wear out before pipes do

In a home from around 1960, the fixtures have often been replaced at least once. Faucets, toilet parts and disposals wear out in years, not decades, and they cause most of the small leaks a homeowner sees.

A dripping faucet or running toilet wastes water and is usually quick to fix. Waiting makes the part and the surrounding area worse.

Everyday repairs we hear about

Running toilet

A worn flapper or fill valve is a common cause. Our toilet repair page explains the usual fixes.

Dripping faucet

A drip is usually a worn cartridge or washer. Faucet repair is often a short visit.

Jammed disposal

Do not put a hand inside. A garbage disposal repair visit can free or replace the unit.

Slow floor drain

Split-level homes often have a lower level with a floor drain and laundry. Pour water into it now and then so the trap stays full.

A note on lower levels

If your home has a lower level, know where its floor drain is and keep the area clear. In a sewer backup, water appears at the lowest drain first.

Wyoming Public Works accepts responsibility for the city sewer mains and the connections within the right of way, and says to call them before a plumber for a sewer backup at 616-530-7260. If the blockage is on the private side, you hire and pay a plumber.
