A failing water line under the yard can flood a basement or drain your pressure to a trickle. Our 24/7 line takes these calls, and you can close the main valve and take a photo of the meter while you wait for a plumber.

The water service line is the buried pipe that carries water from the street main, through the meter, and into your house. Water service line repair in Wyoming covers patching a leak, replacing a failed section or replacing the whole line.

Wet yard, spinning meter, no pressure: what each one means

Service line trouble shows up outside the walls first. These are the signs we hear about most often.

A soggy or sinking patch of lawn

Ground that stays wet in a dry week or settles into a dip may be sitting over a leaking line. Water follows the pipe trench, so the wet spot is not always above the break.

A meter that moves with the house off

If the dial turns after you close the main valve inside, the leak is between the meter and the valve. That buried stretch is exactly what this service covers.

Pressure that drops across the house

Weak flow at every tap, not just one, suggests the problem is upstream of your plumbing. A partly broken line or a crushed section can starve the whole house. Old galvanized piping inside can do the same, which is a repiping question.

Dirty or discolored water

Rust-colored or cloudy water, especially after the pressure drops, can point to corroded pipe or a break that pulls debris into the line.

Water in a basement near the wall where the line enters

Damp or flowing water at the foundation wall near the entry point suggests the line has failed close to the house.

What Wyoming's utility wants you to know before you dig

Wyoming's utility department handles water meters and inspects water service lines, so a replacement is not something to do quietly on a weekend. Ask about inspection before the trench is backfilled, because covering the pipe too early can mean digging it up again.

Water leaking in the street or on the lawn goes to Public Works, which is on call 24/7. Leaks at the meter go to the Utility Business Office at 616-530-7389 in business hours and to Public Works after hours. That quick call tells you whether the failed section is yours to pay for.

Permits for a buried water line

Replacing a water service line is permit work in Wyoming, and the city inspects water service lines. The pipe and joints must meet the 2021 Michigan Plumbing Code, based on the 2021 IPC.

Call MISS DIG 811 before any digging. Which stretch is yours depends on the meter and right-of-way, so confirm with the Utility Business Office at 616-530-7389 before hiring.

How a buried line is found and fixed

Digging blind is expensive. We locate the problem first, then open the smallest trench that gets the job done.

  1. 1

    Confirm the loss

    We use the meter test and a pressure check to prove the water is leaving between the meter and the house.

  2. 2

    Pinpoint the break

    Acoustic listening equipment and sometimes tracer gas narrow the location so the excavation is short.

  3. 3

    Mark utilities and plan the dig

    MISS DIG 811 must be called before digging so buried gas, electric and other lines are marked. This is a legal step and keeps the crew safe.

  4. 4

    Repair or replace

    A short section is spliced with proper fittings. A badly corroded, undersized or lead line is replaced in full.

  5. 5

    Pressure test and inspect

    We test the new line, arrange inspection with the city, and restore the trench.

Repair or full replacement: how to choose

A spot repair is cheaper today, but not always cheaper over time. If the failure is a split inside the house, burst pipe repair is the right page.

SituationLikely choiceReason
One clean break on an otherwise sound lineRepairThe rest of the pipe may last
Old galvanized steel with repeated leaksReplaceCorrosion keeps producing new failures
Lead line, or a line material you cannot confirmReplaceRemoves the lead question permanently
Line crushed by roots or heavy loadsReplace the damaged runPatches do not restore full flow
Low pressure with an undersized old pipeReplaceA larger new line can restore flow

Lead service lines: ask, do not guess

Some older water lines in the country were made of lead, and utilities keep an inventory of the material used on each service line. The City of Wyoming links to information on its water page, so check it or call the utility and ask what material your line is made of. We do not guess based on the age of a house.

For reference, Kentwood's 2024 water quality report lists none of its service connections as lead. That is a neighboring system, not a statement about your address, so confirm yours with the city.

Low pressure is not always the line

A weak shower is more likely a clogged aerator, a failed pressure regulator or a partly closed valve than a broken service line. We check pressure at an outside hose bib and at the main before recommending any excavation.

If pressure is low throughout the house, was fine last month and the meter moves when everything is off, the buried line becomes the top suspect. In that case leak detection confirms the break before any trench is opened.

Permits and neighbors outside the city

A permit is needed in the city before replacing water supply pipes, and the permit holder schedules inspections with at least 24 hours' notice. Plan the dig and the backfill around that call so you are not left with an open trench.

Homes in neighboring Walker are on a different water system, where the utility builds the line to the right-of-way and the owner hires a plumber from there to the house. If your address is outside Wyoming, ask your own utility who owns which section.
