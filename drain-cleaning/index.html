Drain cleaning in Wyoming means removing whatever is blocking a household drain line so water flows freely again. That blockage might be grease, hair, soap scum, food scraps or roots, and the right tool depends on where it sits and what it is made of. Drain backup tonight: water rising in a sink or tub

A drain that is rising rather than draining is a different problem from one that is merely slow. Standing water that climbs when you flush, run a washer or take a shower means the blockage is stopping the whole line, and more water sent down will only land on your floor.

Evening and weekend calls are common because that is when a household uses the most water. We take those calls the same way we take a Tuesday morning one, and we book a same-day appointment when one is available.

The kitchen sink that will not drain

The kitchen sink is the most common drain we are asked to clear, and it is usually a grease problem. Hot cooking fat poured down the drain runs as a liquid, then cools on the pipe wall and catches coffee grounds, rice, pasta and food bits. Over months the pipe narrows from the inside until a single dish load stops it.

Where the clog sits matters. If only the sink backs up, the blockage is typically in the trap beneath it or in the first stretch of horizontal pipe behind the wall. A cable machine reaches that section well and breaks through the plug without much disruption.

A double sink with a disposal adds one more place to look. If water from the dishwasher comes up in the second basin, the blockage sits past the point where the two lines join. If the disposal hums but nothing moves, the issue may be the disposal itself, and our garbage disposal repair service covers that side.

Hot water and dish soap rarely fix a true grease clog, because the fat re-hardens a few feet down the line. A mechanical clearing removes the buildup instead of pushing it along. We also tell you what to keep out of the drain afterward so it does not return in a few weeks.

Is it one fixture or the whole house

Before any machine comes out, we work out how far the blockage reaches. It changes the price, the tool and the urgency.

  1. 1

    Test the other drains

    Run water in a fixture far from the problem, such as a bathroom sink while the kitchen is blocked. If it drains normally, the clog is likely local to the first fixture.

  2. 2

    Watch the lowest drain

    Water that surfaces in a basement floor drain or shower when you use an upstairs fixture points to the main line, not a branch. The lowest opening is where trouble shows first.

  3. 3

    Listen to the toilets

    Gurgling in a toilet when the washer drains, or a bowl that rises and falls, suggests air is struggling past a blockage in the larger line.

  4. 4

    Decide house side or street side

    A clog inside your own plumbing is yours to clear. A backup that starts on the sewer main in the street is handled differently, as explained below.

  5. 5

    Clear and confirm

    We clear the line, run water to check the flow, and tell you what we pulled out. If the same spot keeps clogging, we recommend a camera look instead of repeating the cleaning.

If the backup may be on the city side

Wyoming Public Works is responsible for the sanitary sewer mains and the residential connections within the right of way. If a backup starts on that side, Public Works coordinates the repair at no cost to you, and it keeps staff on call 24/7 at 616-530-7260. The city asks that you call Public Works before you call a plumber.

If several fixtures back up at once, make that call first. When the city finds the problem on your private side, you hire a plumber and pay, and we are glad to take that call afterward. The same logic is covered on our sewer line cleaning page for main-line work.

Is clearing a drain permit work?

Running a cable through a clog does not replace any pipe, so it normally sits outside the permit rule. Wyoming requires permits for replacing drains, vent and water supply pipes, and those repairs must follow the 2021 Michigan Plumbing Code, based on the 2021 IPC.

If cleaning exposes a collapsed section, expect the fix to need a permit and a licensed plumber.

Why older Wyoming homes clog differently

Much of Wyoming was built during the post-war boom of the 1950s, and many houses from that era still carry original drain lines. Those pipes may be cast iron or clay in the sewer connection, and they develop rough interiors and joints that catch debris. If your home is from that period, a stubborn recurring clog often means the pipe surface itself is the problem.

That does not mean every old house needs new pipe. It means a camera inspection is worth considering when cleaning gives only short relief. We also take calls from neighboring areas such as Grandville, which Wyoming wraps around on the east and south, and the details are on our Grandville plumbing page.

Fix it yourself or call us

A plunger or hand auger is fine when

  • Only one fixture is slow and the others drain well.
  • The clog started after something obvious, like a pile of hair.
  • Water is draining, just slowly, and nothing is rising.
  • You can reach the trap or stopper without tools.

Call a plumber when

  • Water is rising or overflowing from any drain.
  • Two or more fixtures are slow at once.
  • A floor drain gurgles or smells of sewage.
  • You have already tried a plunger and a store-bought auger.
