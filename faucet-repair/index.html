Faucet and sink repair in Wyoming covers the spout, handles, cartridge, supply lines, shutoff valves and drain parts of a sink, tub or shower. Most leaks come from a seal, cartridge or connector rather than the fixture itself.

Why faucets leak: seals, cartridges and washers

Every faucet controls flow with a moving part that seals against a seat. In older compression faucets that is a rubber washer pressed against the seat, and it wears into a groove that lets a drip through. Newer single-handle faucets use a cartridge, a plastic or brass sleeve with internal ports that slides or rotates to mix hot and cold.

When the cartridge's seals wear, the faucet drips from the spout or leaks around the handle. When mineral scale roughens the seat, the new part may seal badly from the start, so cleaning the seat matters as much as the part.

Safety steps before you open a faucet

  • ✓Close both stop valves under the sink, or the main valve if there are none.
  • ✓Open the faucet to bleed off pressure before loosening anything.
  • ✓Plug the drain so small parts cannot fall in.
  • ✓Let hot water cool, because a hot cartridge can scald.
  • ✓Keep outlets and cords away from the work area.

How we find the leak and fix it

Diagnosis is mostly about separating a fixture leak from a supply or drain leak.

  1. 1

    Dry it and run it

    We dry every connection, then run the fixture and watch where the first bead of water appears.

  2. 2

    Check the supply side

    We test the shutoff valves and braided lines, which fail more often than the faucet body.

  3. 3

    Open the fixture

    We pull the handle and cartridge, inspect the seat and seals, and note scale or corrosion.

  4. 4

    Check the drain side

    A cabinet that gets wet only when the sink drains points to the trap or tailpiece, not the faucet.

  5. 5

    Confirm before closing up

    We run hot and cold, test the shutoffs, and check for drips again after pressure has built.

Showers, tubs and sink drains

Shower valve

A valve that runs hot, drips from the showerhead after shutoff, or leaks behind the handle usually needs a new cartridge. If it is stuck, the stem or valve body may be at fault.

Tub spout

Water pouring from the spout while the shower is on means the diverter inside is worn. Many spouts simply unscrew and swap.

Sink drain

A bathroom sink that drains slowly often holds hair around the pop-up. Clearing it takes a few minutes, though a deeper clog calls for drain cleaning.

Tub and shower drain

Standing water in a shower is a drain issue, not a faucet one. Hair and soap form a plug that a drain snake clears.

DIY or call: a simple line to draw

  • ✓Do clean aerators by unscrewing them and soaking them in vinegar.
  • ✓Do replace a braided supply line if you can reach the shutoff and it works.
  • ✓Do not take apart a shower valve without turning off the water at the main.
  • ✓Do not tighten a connection hard enough to crush the fitting.
  • ✓Call if the shutoff leaks, will not turn, or the cabinet floor is soft.
  • ✓Call if you see a stain on the ceiling below a bathroom, which suggests a hidden leak.

Adding or replacing fixtures

Swapping a faucet for a matching one is a simple job. Relocating a sink or adding one is fixture work, and the pairing of a kitchen faucet and a garbage disposal often shows up together on kitchen updates. Bring the new faucet's box with you so we can check hole spacing and supply fittings before the old one comes out.

When a fixture job becomes permit work

Swapping a cartridge or washer is routine repair. Adding fixtures or replacing supply pipe is different: Wyoming requires a plumbing permit for that, under the 2021 Michigan Plumbing Code based on the 2021 IPC.

Stop valves and supply lines are the gray area, so ask before you start. A licensed plumber handles the permit, and an owner-resident may pull one personally.
