A boiler heats water and sends it through pipes to radiators or baseboards, which is why hydronic heat feels steadier than forced air. Boiler repair in Wyoming usually involves the water side: pressure, air, circulator pumps, valves and the expansion tank. Many Michigan homes depend on one all winter, so a cold house is worth a call the same day.

Gas or carbon monoxide first, repair second

If you smell gas, leave the building immediately, do not switch lights or devices on or off, and call 911 or DTE Energy, the area's natural gas utility, from outside. If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, get everyone out into fresh air and call 911.

Only after the utility or fire department clears the house should you call a plumber. Boilers burn fuel, and flue or combustion problems are safety issues, not repairs to attempt yourself.

What no heat usually means

A cold house with a running boiler is a different problem from a boiler that will not start.

Boiler will not fire

Low water pressure, a tripped limit, a dead thermostat or a failed ignition can all stop it. Check the thermostat settings and the breaker before calling.

Boiler runs but radiators stay cold

Air trapped in the pipes, a failed circulator or a closed zone valve keeps hot water from circulating.

One zone is cold

A stuck zone valve or a trapped air pocket in that loop is typical. The rest of the house heating normally narrows it down.

Heat is weak

Scale buildup, a clogged strainer or low water in the system reduces output.

Boiler cycles constantly

Oversized equipment, a bad thermostat or pressure swings cause short cycling. It wastes fuel and wears parts.

Pressure, the expansion tank and the relief valve

A hydronic system is a sealed loop. The boiler holds a set pressure, and a small expansion tank absorbs the extra volume as water heats. When the tank loses its air charge, pressure spikes every time the boiler fires, and the relief valve opens and drips.

A dripping relief valve is a symptom, not the disease. Replacing the valve alone usually brings the drip back within weeks if the tank or fill valve is the cause.

Safety steps before you touch the boiler

  • ✓If you smell gas, leave the house at once and call 911 or DTE Energy from outside.
  • ✓If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, get everyone outdoors and call 911.
  • ✓Turn off the boiler's power switch before checking anything.
  • ✓Do not use a lighter, match or open flame near the boiler.
  • ✓Never open the relief valve on a hot system, because the discharge can scald.

No heat during a Michigan winter

Michigan winters can freeze pipes in an unheated house, and a failed boiler during a hard freeze puts the plumbing at risk as well as your comfort. If the boiler is down and the house is cooling, open cabinet doors under sinks, let one tap drip, and use safe supplemental heat. Call early, because a part may need to be sourced.

Many Wyoming homes date from the post-war development of the 1950s, so some still run original-era heating with older radiators and cast iron boilers. Those systems can last, but old piping and valves are more likely to leak or seize. When you call, mention the age and type of the boiler so dispatch can plan the visit.

Repair now or plan a replacement

Repair is sensible when

  • The failed part is a pump, valve or sensor.
  • The heat exchanger is sound.
  • The boiler is efficient enough for your bills.
  • Parts are still available.

Plan a replacement when

  • The heat exchanger is leaking.
  • Repairs are piling up each winter.
  • Parts are discontinued.
  • The boiler is oversized or unsafe.

Permit and license rules for boiler piping

Replacing water supply pipes around a boiler needs a plumbing permit in Wyoming before work starts. The piping must follow the 2021 Michigan Plumbing Code, built on the 2021 IPC.

The person doing the work must be licensed by the State of Michigan for that trade, or be the owner who lives in the home. The permit holder books the inspection with at least 24 hours' notice, so ask your plumber who is handling that step.
