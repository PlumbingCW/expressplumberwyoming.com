Replacing a water heater in Clyde Park

A tank water heater is one of the most common big plumbing jobs in older small homes. Wyoming lists hot water heaters among the work that needs a plumbing permit, so the replacement is not a casual swap.

Wyoming Building Inspections (616-530-7285) requires a plumbing permit before work starts on hot water heaters and on replacing drain, vent and water supply pipes.

A plumber who pulls the permit also arranges the inspection, which is worth asking about before you agree to the price.

How a water heater swap usually goes

  1. 1

    Check the old tank

    Look for rust at the base, damp floor or a rumbling noise. Any of these suggests the tank is near the end of its life.

  2. 2

    Choose the type

    Tank, tankless and heat pump units all exist. Water heater replacement covers the choices and what fits a small utility space.

  3. 3

    Permit and inspection

    The permit comes before the work. An inspector confirms the installation afterward.

  4. 4

    Install and test

    The plumber connects water and gas or power, checks for leaks and shows you the shutoff.

Hard water and your heater

City and neighboring utility reports put Lake Michigan water hardness in the 140s ppm, or roughly 8 to 9 grains per gallon. That is moderately hard, so scale slowly builds inside a tank.

Flushing a tank once a year is a common maintenance step. A water softener is a separate decision and depends on how you feel about the water at your taps. Test your tap before you buy anything.
