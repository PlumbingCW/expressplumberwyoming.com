Water softener and filtration work in Wyoming starts with the mineral content of Lake Michigan water, which has supplied the city since 1966. A softener swaps hardness minerals for sodium or potassium. A filter removes particles, chlorine taste or odor, and the two do different jobs.

This is planned work. If a softener has stopped regenerating, leaks at the brine tank or leaves scale on your fixtures, we can service it, replace it or help you decide whether you need one at all.

How hard is the water here?

City and neighboring utility reports put hardness in the 140s ppm, roughly 8 to 9 grains per gallon, so test your tap. The city's own water page lists calcium at 39 mg/L and magnesium at 13 mg/L, and those two minerals are what make water hard. Water in that range leaves chalky rings on glass, mineral crust on faucet aerators and a film on shower doors, which is the practical signal that a softener could help.

Hardness is not a health problem. It shortens the life of water heaters, dishwashers and fixtures and makes soap less effective. Ask the utility for its annual water quality report, which the city mails before July 1 each year, and compare it with a home test kit result.

Softener, filter or both

Each device handles a different part of what comes from the tap.

DeviceWhat it doesWhat it does not do
Water softenerRemoves calcium and magnesium that cause scaleDoes not filter particles or improve taste
Carbon filterReduces chlorine taste and odorDoes not reduce hardness
Sediment filterCatches sand and rust particlesDoes not change hardness or taste
Softener plus filterHandles scale and taste togetherCosts more and needs two sets of maintenance
Drinking water filter at one tapTreats drinking and cooking water onlyLeaves the rest of the house untreated

Plumbing in a softener: code and the maker's manual

Connecting a softener to the water line falls under the 2021 Michigan Plumbing Code, based on the 2021 IPC, and installs that alter supply piping are permit work in Wyoming. The regeneration drain connection matters too.

Follow the unit's manufacturer instructions for salt, bypass and drain settings. Start with a tap test, since local reports put hardness in the 140s ppm.

Signs a softener has stopped doing its job

Scale is back

Chalky buildup on fixtures and glass returns after a period of clean results. The resin bed may be exhausted or the control valve may not be regenerating.

No salt use

A brine tank that never goes down means the unit is not drawing brine. A salt bridge, a crust that forms across the salt, is a common cause.

Water feels slick or salty

A unit that rinses poorly can leave residue. The cause may be a stuck valve or a clogged injector.

Leaks or standing water

Leaks at the brine tank, the drain line or the bypass valve need prompt service. Moisture around the unit is not normal.

Low pressure after the softener

Resin beads that escape or a clogged screen can restrict flow through the unit.

Repairing an old unit versus fitting a new one

Repair is reasonable when

  • The control valve or injector is the failure.
  • The tank is sound.
  • The system is the right size for the household.
  • Service parts are available.

Replace when

  • The tank is cracked or leaking.
  • The resin is exhausted and the unit is old.
  • The household has grown since it was installed.
  • The model is a discontinued conditioner design.

Scale and tankless heaters

Tankless water heaters heat water quickly through a small heat exchanger, and scale collects inside it. Even moderately hard water coats the exchanger over time, reducing flow and efficiency. Many tankless owners flush the unit regularly, and a softener upstream reduces how often. See our tankless water heater page for service, and our water heater repair page if a tank unit is acting up.

What the city says about PFAS

The city reports sampling that found less than 5 ppt of PFOA and PFOS. A softener is not designed to remove those compounds, and we do not make claims beyond the city's own statement. If you have a specific concern about your water, ask the utility or a certified testing lab and then decide what treatment fits.

Installing a new system

A new softener needs a spot near the main water line, a floor drain or standpipe for backwash, and a power outlet. Piping changes are our part of the job, and bypass valves let you shut off treated water if you ever need to. Our installation service covers new treatment equipment, and we also help homes in Grandville with the same needs.

Safety steps around brine and bypass

  • ✓Unplug the softener before opening the control head.
  • ✓Set the bypass valve before touching any plumbing.
  • ✓Keep salt and brine away from children and pets.
  • ✓Never mix cleaning chemicals in the brine tank.
  • ✓Wipe up spills at once, because wet salt floors are slippery.
