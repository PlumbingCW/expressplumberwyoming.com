Repiping is planned work, and the best time to plan it is before the next leak. If your pipes keep failing, our team can walk the house and lay out options. Burst pipes still reach our 24/7 line.

Whole-house repiping in Wyoming means replacing the water supply lines inside your home with new copper or PEX, either all at once or room by room. It does not include the buried line from the street or the drain system.

Signs the pipes in your house are at the end of their run

Repeated leaks in different spots are the clearest sign. A patch on one pinhole is fine, but a second and third pinhole on the same run tell you the metal is thinning everywhere, not just at one point.

Other signs include brown or rusty water when you first open a tap, pressure that is weak at several fixtures, and flaking or crusty buildup on exposed pipe. Any one of these can have a smaller cause, so we test before recommending a full replacement.

Mid-century homes and the pipes inside them

Wyoming's fastest growth came in the post-war 1950s, after US-131 was approved, and many homes from that period still have original or early replacement plumbing. Some older houses were piped in galvanized steel, which corrodes from the inside, narrows over time and tends to lose pressure before it leaks.

If your home dates from that era, ask us to look at the pipe where it enters the basement and at exposed runs. Seeing the material on the first visit lets you plan repiping before a failure forces a rushed one. A magnet and a small scratch test can help sort galvanized from copper, though we confirm on site.

The permit and the inspector on a repipe

A repipe replaces water supply pipes, which puts it squarely under Wyoming's permit rule and the 2021 Michigan Plumbing Code (based on the 2021 IPC). The permit holder schedules inspections with at least 24 hours' notice.

A Michigan plumbing contractor must hold a master plumber license or employ a master plumber as representative. Ask who holds it for your repipe, and verify with LARA before pipe is cut.

Copper, PEX and galvanized steel side by side

Most repipes use copper or PEX. Galvanized is mostly what you are replacing, not what you are installing.

CopperLong track record, rigid, handles heat wellCosts more, takes more labor, can pit in aggressive water
PEXFlexible, fewer fittings, quieter, quicker to run in wallsNot for outdoor sun exposure, needs approved fittings

We recommend a material based on your house, your water and your budget, not on a fixed preference.

Whole house or one section at a time

Repipe the whole house when

  • Leaks keep showing up in different rooms
  • Most of the piping is galvanized
  • You are already opening walls for a remodel
  • Pressure and water color are poor everywhere

Replace a section when

  • One run or one bathroom is failing
  • The rest was updated in a past project
  • Budget needs the work spread over time
  • The main trouble is a single leaking branch

How a repipe is phased so you can keep living there

Repiping is disruptive but rarely requires leaving the house for long. A phased plan keeps water on as much as possible.

  1. 1

    Survey and plan

    We trace the existing lines, decide where new pipe will run and mark where access holes are needed.

  2. 2

    Permit and scheduling

    The permit is filed before work starts. We schedule around inspection windows so walls are not closed before approval.

  3. 3

    Rough in the new lines

    New supply runs go in beside the old ones, usually through basements, closets and the least disruptive routes.

  4. 4

    Switch over and test

    We connect the new lines to the main and fixtures, then pressure test every joint before opening the valve.

  5. 5

    Close up and inspect

    The inspector checks the work, old pipe is capped or removed, and access holes are ready for a drywall contractor.

Permit and inspection notes for the city

Wyoming requires a plumbing permit before replacement of water supply pipes, and the state requires a licensed plumber for this work unless the owner lives in the home. Contractors register with the city before applying, and the permit holder schedules inspections with at least 24 hours' notice, so build that wait into the timeline.

We pull the permit and handle the scheduling. Walls and ceilings stay open until the inspector signs off, so tell any painter or drywall contractor to wait for that date. Our team also works in Kentwood and nearby cities.

What repiping does not fix

New supply pipe will not cure a clogged drain, a failing water heater or a damaged line under the yard. It also does not replace drywall or paint, which a drywall contractor or painter handles after inspection.

If your biggest problem is a leak you cannot find, start with leak detection so we know whether repiping is really the answer.

Pairing a repipe with other plumbing work

If you are already remodeling a kitchen or bath, repiping the supply lines at the same time saves opening the walls twice. Ask about new shutoff valves, a new water heater connection and any fixtures you plan to add through plumbing installation.

That bundling matters most for older homes where a single remodel often exposes pipes that were never meant to last this long. Even if you only replace one section now, we can leave tees and valves where future runs will connect.
