New plumbing goes in once, and the choices you make at the start are hard to change after drywall. This page covers what we install, how permits fit in, and where our work stops. Genuine emergencies still reach the 24/7 line.

Plumbing installation in Wyoming means putting in new water supply, drain, vent and gas lines and connecting fixtures and appliances to them. It applies to remodels, additions and new builds.

What we put in

Most installation calls fall into a few kinds of projects. If yours is different, tell dispatch and we will say whether it fits.

Kitchen and bath fixtures

Sinks, faucets, toilets, tubs, showers and disposals, with supply lines and shutoff valves matched to the fixture.

Laundry and utility hookups

Washer boxes, standpipes, utility sinks and drains for basements, mudrooms and garages.

Rough-in for remodels and additions

Supply, drain and vent runs set in open walls and floors before the finish work begins.

New construction plumbing

Rough-in and finish work for new homes and accessory spaces, coordinated with the builder's schedule.

Water heaters and softener connections

Plumbing for a new or relocated water heater, a softener or a filtration unit.

Choosing fixtures that will not cause trouble later

Buy fixtures with the installation in mind. A wall-mounted faucet needs different rough-in depth than a deck-mounted one, and a one-piece toilet may need a different flange position than a two-piece model. Bring the box or the specification sheet to the first visit, and we check it against the space.

Look at the water supply too. Moderately hard water in this area can leave scale on fixtures with fine aerators and spray heads, so ask how a fixture handles buildup. A fixture that clogs in a year is a poor bargain, and a toilet repair call for a cheap fill valve costs more than choosing a better one at the start.

For a new water heater, plan the venting, the gas connection and the drain path before you pick a model. Our water heater installation work covers the plumbing that goes with it, and doing both projects together avoids cutting the same wall twice.

How permits work in the city and the next town over

In Wyoming, permit applications are filed online or on paper, the invoice is paid before the permit is issued, and the work must be done by someone licensed by the State of Michigan or by the owner who lives in the home. Contractors register first. Building Inspections is at 1155 28th Street SW, so a printed application can be dropped off in person.

If you are building in Grandville, the process differs because Grandville uses an outside inspection office for plumbing permits. Check with the city before assuming a Wyoming rule applies there, since the wrong application will delay your start date.

Do-it-yourself, a plumber, or both

Owners who live in their home may be allowed to do plumbing work themselves with a permit, and many people do the demolition and the finish while hiring us for the part that must pass inspection. That can save money on a remodel.

The risk is gas, shared drains and any connection that can flood a room. If you are unsure, hire out the rough-in and keep the finish work, or call us before you cut anything. Michigan follows the 2021 Michigan Plumbing Code, so materials and venting need to match it.

Gas lines: safety comes before any plumbing call

If you smell gas, leave the building immediately and call 911 or your gas utility before you call a plumber. Do not turn lights or switches on or off, use a lighter, or start a car in an attached garage. Natural gas in Wyoming is supplied by DTE Energy, so use the number on your bill or the emergency line.

We install, extend and test gas piping for appliances such as ranges, dryers, fireplaces and outdoor grills, and we inspect existing lines. We never make a gas promise to arrive by a set time. Gas work is never a do-it-yourself job, because a poor joint can leak where you cannot smell it. Shutoffs, flexible connectors and appliance hookups need pressure testing.

Licensing and permits for new fixtures

New plumbing is where the code matters most. The 2021 Michigan Plumbing Code, based on the 2021 IPC and effective March 12, 2024, governs new construction, added fixtures and replaced pipes, and Wyoming requires the permit before work starts.

The work must be done by someone licensed by the State of Michigan for it, or by the owner who lives in the home. Michigan licenses apprentices, journey and master plumbers, and plumbing contractors, so ask who will be on your job.

What we do not do

We are a plumbing company. We do not wire electrical outlets, build structure, install HVAC, or do tile, drywall and cabinetry. For a remodel those trades work alongside us, and we coordinate the plumbing timing.

Electricity in Wyoming comes from Consumers Energy, and electrical work needs its own permit through the city. If a new appliance needs a dedicated circuit, an electrician must do that portion.
