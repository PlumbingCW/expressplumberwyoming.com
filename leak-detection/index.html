Leak detection in Wyoming means finding exactly where water is escaping from a supply line, drain or fixture before anyone cuts into a wall, floor or yard. The goal is one small, accurate repair instead of a large guess.

Start with the symptom: what your house is telling you

Most leaks announce themselves in ordinary ways long before water pools anywhere. Match what you are seeing to the likely cause, then use the sections below to see how we confirm it.

Meter moving with everything off

If the dial or low-flow indicator keeps turning while no tap, toilet or appliance is running, water is leaving somewhere. The leak could be inside the house, in a fixture, or on the pipe between the meter and the foundation.

A bill that jumped for no reason

A sudden increase with the same household habits usually points to a steady leak or a toilet that refills on its own. Check the previous few bills side by side before you assume the utility made a mistake.

A brown ring on the ceiling

Ceiling stains that grow after someone showers upstairs suggest a drain or supply leak above. Stains that grow after rain suggest a roof problem, which is a roofer's job and not ours.

A warm spot on the floor

A patch of floor that feels warm underfoot can mean a hot supply line leaking beneath it. The same patch may smell musty or sound faintly like running water at night.

Low pressure that came on gradually

Pressure that fades across weeks often means a pipe is losing water before it reaches the fixtures. Corroded fittings and partly closed valves can do the same thing, so we rule those out as well.

The meter test you can do before we arrive

A meter is the most honest leak detector in the house. It takes a short quiet stretch and tells us whether the leak is inside or outside.

  1. 1

    Stop all water use

    Turn off taps, dishwasher, washing machine and ice maker. Pause any irrigation controller and make sure nobody flushes during the test.

  2. 2

    Read the meter

    Open the lid and note the small leak indicator and the sweep dial. Take a clear photo so you can compare it later.

  3. 3

    Wait and look again

    Come back after a stretch of time with the water still off. If the indicator is spinning or the reading has changed, water is escaping.

  4. 4

    Close the main and recheck

    Shut the home's main valve and watch the meter again. If it stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving with the main closed, the leak is on the supply side before the valve.

Meter leaks and who the city wants you to call

Wyoming's own rules treat the meter as a dividing line. Leaks at the meter go to the Utility Business Office at 616-530-7389 during business hours, and to Public Works after hours. Fixture and sprinkler leaks inside the home are the homeowner's responsibility.

That split saves you money and time. If water is bubbling out of the meter pit itself, call the city first. Leaks at the meter are handled through the city's Utility Business Office or Public Works, so ask them before you pay for a repair. If the meter test shows a leak but the pit is dry, call us and we will find it on your side of the line.

Wet ceilings, damp walls and attic stains

Water travels. A drip at the ceiling is rarely under the pipe that is leaking, because water follows framing, ducts and wiring before it shows itself. We trace the stain back along the likely routes instead of opening drywall right under it.

For hidden wall and ceiling leaks we use a moisture meter, thermal imaging to show cooler wet areas, and a pressure test on the suspect line. A typical call starts with the stain, then a check of the bathroom or laundry above it, then an isolation test that closes off one branch at a time.

If the leak turns out to be a failed pipe rather than a slow drip, burst pipe repair takes over. We stop and fix the plumbing source. We do not dry out, remove or rebuild wet drywall and insulation, and we do not repair roofs. Your insurer or a restoration company handles that side, and photos taken before we start help your claim.

Who needs to know before a leak repair starts

Finding the leak needs no permit. Fixing it can. Under Wyoming's rules, replaced water supply pipes need a plumbing permit before work starts, and the job must meet the 2021 Michigan Plumbing Code, based on the 2021 IPC.

If detection shows the leak is at the meter or in the street, the city handles it and no permit falls on you. That is one reason to locate the leak first.

Crawlspaces and under-house leaks

Many Wyoming homes from the post-war building boom sit over a basement, but some have a crawlspace under an addition or a porch. A leak there is easier to reach than one under concrete, yet it often goes unnoticed for months.

Signs include a musty smell, sagging insulation, rust on hangers and fittings, and a damp patch on the soil below a drain or supply line. We inspect the visible runs, test them under pressure, and look closely at joints, valves and old repairs where the pipe changes material.

Buried line leaks between the meter and the house

The pipe that carries water from the meter to your foundation is out of sight, which is why its leaks last so long. Look for a patch of lawn that stays green or soggy in dry weather, a sunken spot, or a meter that moves even when the main valve inside is closed.

We narrow it down with acoustic listening equipment and, where useful, tracer gas that escapes at the break and is picked up at the surface. Before any digging, someone must call MISS DIG 811 so buried utilities are marked. If the line itself has failed, repairing or replacing the service line is the next step.

Slab leaks: real, but not the whole story here

Slab leaks are pipes leaking in or under a concrete floor. They are common in some parts of the country, but many homes here have basements, so most hidden leaks are in walls, ceilings and exposed basement runs rather than under concrete.

Slabs do appear under garages, additions, enclosed porches and some slab-on-grade homes. If you have a warm floor spot, a hissing sound at night or unexplained meter movement in one of those areas, we test for a slab leak using acoustic and thermal equipment before suggesting anything.

Water is actively escaping or the meter will not stop

Active leaks get worse fast, and a cracked fitting can release a lot of water in an hour. Slow the water down first, then call our 24/7 line so dispatch can book the next available plumber, with same-day appointments when available.

  • 1Close the main shutoff valve, usually near the meter or where the line enters the house.
  • 2Open a low tap to drain pressure from the pipes.
  • 3Photograph the meter dial, the leak and any stains with the time visible.
  • 4Move belongings away from the wet area and lift rugs off the floor.
  • 5Call your insurance company if water has reached walls, ceilings or flooring.

Irrigation and pool leaks, honestly

Sprinkler leaks are a common reason a meter moves when nobody is home. A cracked head, a split lateral or a stuck valve can run for days. We can check the plumbing that feeds the system, but irrigation zones and controllers belong to a landscaper or irrigation contractor, and the city treats sprinkler leaks as the homeowner's responsibility.

Pool and spa leaks are a mixed bag. We look at the supply and return plumbing and the fill line to the pool. Liner, shell and pump repairs are a pool service company's work, and we will say so plainly if the leak is outside our scope.
