A larger yard changes the plumbing questions. This page looks at outdoor faucets, the line that brings water into the house and sump pump basics. These are general notes, not findings about specific homes. Call (855) 575-2890 to book a visit.

Outdoor faucets and the first freeze

Hose bibs on the outside of the house can split when water freezes inside the pipe. In Michigan that makes fall shutdown a routine task.

Disconnect hoses before the first hard freeze and shut off the interior valve to the faucet if there is one. If a faucet drips after you turn it off, have it looked at by someone who does faucet repair.

A frozen outdoor pipe often shows up only in spring, when the thaw turns a small crack into a steady leak inside the wall. If you see a damp spot near the faucet after the first warm days, shut off the water and call for a repair before the wall stays wet.

Yard-related plumbing to know about

Water service line

This is the line from the street to the house. Wet spots in the yard or a sudden bill increase can point to a leak, so water service line repair is a call worth making.

Before you dig

Call MISS DIG 811 before any digging so buried utilities are marked.

Sump pumps

If you have a basement, check that a sump pump runs and drains away from the house. Test it before spring thaw.

Sprinklers

City Public Works says sprinkler leaks inside the home are the homeowner's job, so shut off the line before winter.

Who is responsible for a water leak

Wyoming Public Works handles leaks in the street, at hydrants and at the meter after hours. Fixture and sprinkler leaks inside your property are your responsibility.

If you are not sure where the leak starts, call Public Works at 616-530-7260 first. They can tell you if the city side is involved.
