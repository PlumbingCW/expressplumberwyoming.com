A tankless water heater in Wyoming heats water only when a tap opens, using a gas burner or electric elements and a heat exchanger. There is no tank to store hot water, so the unit lives or dies by its heat exchanger staying clean and its gas and vent being sized right.

We flush and descale tankless units, install new ones, and repair units that throw errors or run lukewarm. You get a clear price before work starts. If your unit stops working in the middle of the night, our 24/7 line is available.

Flushing and descaling: the job hard water makes necessary

Lake Michigan water in this area is moderately hard. City and neighboring utility reports put hardness in the 140s ppm, roughly 8 to 9 grains per gallon, so test your tap. A tankless unit pushes that water through narrow passages in the heat exchanger, where minerals bake onto the metal as scale.

Scale acts like insulation. The exchanger must run hotter to move heat through it, flow drops, and the unit may overheat or shut down. Left alone, it shortens the life of the heater and raises gas use. Because of this, many manufacturers call for a flush on a regular schedule, and yearly is a sensible starting point for homes with this water.

What a descale visit involves

  1. 1

    Shut off and isolate

    We close the gas, cut the power and shut the water, then open the service valves on the unit.

  2. 2

    Circulate descaling solution

    A small pump drives a mild acid, usually a food-grade vinegar or a manufacturer-approved cleaner, through the heat exchanger in a loop.

  3. 3

    Rinse and clean the filter

    Fresh water flushes the loop out, and we clean the inlet screen where grit collects.

  4. 4

    Restore and test

    We reopen the valves, restart the unit and check flow, temperature rise and any leaks.

  5. 5

    Advise on prevention

    If scale is heavy, we may suggest a softener or a scale-prevention filter. See water softener and filtration for what each does.

Signs a tankless unit needs attention

Lukewarm or fluctuating water

The classic cold-water sandwich, or a temperature that swings, often traces to scale or a clogged inlet screen.

Error code on the display

Codes are a unit's way of saying which circuit failed. Common groups cover ignition, flame, overheating, flow or venting problems.

Longer wait for hot water

The unit takes longer to fire or never reaches set temperature, which can mean a dirty exchanger or a weak flame.

Noise from the unit

Kettle-like rumbling suggests scale, while repeated clicking suggests ignition trouble.

Water on the floor under the unit

A leak can come from a service valve, a cracked exchanger or a condensate drain on a high-efficiency model.

Installing a tankless heater: what the house must provide

Tankless units need much more gas at once than a tank, because they heat water on demand. An existing half-inch line may be too small, and the meter or regulator may need a look. The gas supplier is DTE Energy, and we coordinate with the meter limits when needed.

Venting is different too. High-efficiency models use sealed plastic pipe that can run out a side wall, while others need stainless steel venting. Combustion air, clearances and a condensate drain are all part of the plan. Compare that with a standard tank in planned water heater installation, which has different needs.

The City of Wyoming lists hot water heaters among permit work, so the permit and inspection fit into a tankless job just as they do for a tank.

Repairing a tankless unit: error codes in plain terms

Every brand has its own code list, and the same number can mean different things between makers. In general, ignition codes point to a dirty flame rod or a gas supply issue, flow codes point to a clogged filter or a bad flow sensor, and overheat codes point to scale or a failing thermistor. We read the code and then test the cause instead of replacing parts at random.

If the unit is failing often, a repair may not pay off. Our water heater repair team can tell you if the heat exchanger is the problem, since that part is expensive and may not be worth fixing on an older unit.

A tankless heater does not remove the need for service

People often hear that tankless units last longer and then skip maintenance. They tend to outlast tanks only when flushed and kept clean. Skipping the flush is the quickest way to shorten the life of a good unit.

Plan the flush for the same time each year, and keep the paperwork, since some warranties ask for proof of maintenance.
