Urbandale is part of the GUB area (Galewood, Urbandale and Burlingame), which the Grand Rapids, Holland and Chicago Railway platted from the 1890s into the 1920s. The population of what became Wyoming grew from 5,702 in 1920 to 16,931 in 1930.

That history is why Urbandale mixes very old and mid-century homes. Sources disagree on exact boundaries, but all place it in the northern part of the city near Chicago Drive and Clyde Park Avenue. Call (855) 575-2890 to book a visit.

What an older house can hide

A home from the early 1900s may have had parts of its plumbing replaced several times. That means a house's age tells you little about the pipes behind the walls. Some lines may be new while others are original.

Instead of guessing, ask a plumber to look at the parts you can reach. A sewer camera shows the drain, and a pressure check shows the supply side.

Write down what you see before you call. Note which fixtures are affected, when the problem started and whether it changes with the weather or the time of day. Those details help narrow the cause and keep a visit short.

Signs worth a closer look

Repeated clogs

If the same drain backs up every few months, a camera inspection can show whether the cause is inside the pipe.

Low water pressure

Pressure that drops at several fixtures can mean a restriction in the supply line.

Discolored water

Brown water after a long idle period can come from pipes or from the city main. Run the tap and note how long it lasts.

Visible corrosion

Green or white crust at joints and valves is a sign to have them checked.

Spot repair or repipe

A single failed section is usually a spot repair. A house with repeated leaks may be worth discussing repiping, but it is a big job and the right call depends on what an inspection shows.

Wyoming Building Inspections (616-530-7285) requires a plumbing permit before work starts on hot water heaters and on replacing drain, vent and water supply pipes.
